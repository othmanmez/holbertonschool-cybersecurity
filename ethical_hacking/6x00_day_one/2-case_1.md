# Case 1 – The Handshake Deal

## 1. What happened

NorthBay Consulting started a penetration test after receiving verbal authorization from the client's CTO. There was no written authorization and other important people in the company, including the CEO and legal department, did not know about the engagement. During the test, the consultants exploited a SQL injection vulnerability and extracted 500 shipping records containing sensitive customer information. The company thought the activity was a real data breach and reported it to the authorities.

## 2. What rules, laws, or professional standards were violated

The main problem was that the testing was started without proper written authorization.

The CTO said that he had authorized the test, but he did not have the proper authority to authorize third-party access to the company's systems. The consultants therefore did not have a clear and legally defensible authorization.

The engagement also involved accessing and extracting personal and sensitive information without a clearly agreed data-handling process.

This was against basic professional penetration testing standards. A penetration test should have a clear written scope and authorization before any testing starts.

## 3. What the correct professional behavior would have been

NorthBay should not have started testing based only on a phone call.

The consulting firm should have:

* Confirmed who had legal authority to authorize the test.
* Obtained written authorization.
* Created a clear scope document.
* Defined which systems could be tested.
* Defined how sensitive data should be handled.
* Confirmed the engagement with the relevant legal and management contacts.

If the client wanted the test to start quickly, the firm could still prepare the documents quickly, but testing should not begin before the authorization was properly signed.

## 4. Clauses that could have prevented this outcome

The pre-engagement documents should have included clauses such as:

### Written Authorization

"Testing may only begin after written authorization has been signed by an authorized representative of the client."

### Authority to Authorize

"The client confirms that the person signing this authorization has the legal and organizational authority to permit the testing activities described in this agreement."

### Scope

"Only the systems, applications, IP addresses, and domains specifically listed in the scope are authorized for testing."

### Sensitive Data

"The testing team must minimize the collection of sensitive data. If sensitive data is accessed accidentally, it must be handled according to the agreed data-handling procedures."

### No Verbal Authorization

"Verbal statements or informal requests do not modify the agreed scope or provide authorization for additional testing activities."
