# Strategic Threat Profile, Nordstrøm Power Group

## Framework choice and rationale
A hybrid model combining PASTA’s business-impact and dependency thinking with MITRE ATT&CK and ATT&CK for ICS is the right fit here. It lets Nordstrøm’s board see the risk in terms of operational continuity, governance, and regulatory exposure, while preserving the adversary-centric technical mapping needed for real scoping decisions across five jurisdictions and multiple energy sub-sectors.

## Group-level profile
Nordstrøm presents as a mid-to-large European energy holding with a high-value, cross-border operational footprint: offshore production, grid-connected generation, trading, gas and hydrogen infrastructure, and a newly acquired German solar utility undergoing integration. The group-level threat picture is therefore not a generic “energy sector” problem. It is a multi-domain target set with exposure across industrial control systems, corporate IT, procurement and trading systems, and a dynamic M&A integration boundary in Germany.

The most material strategic threats are: state-aligned or nation-backed intrusion groups targeting energy and infrastructure dependencies; ransomware and extortion operators focused on operational downtime and payment pressure; insider or third-party abuse through privileged administration, contractor access, and supplier connectivity; and supply-chain compromise affecting industrial automation, SaaS integrations, or service providers. At the group level, the major risk is not a single technology weakness but the combination of heterogeneous assets, differing NIS2 classifications, and uneven control maturity across entities. This creates a broad attack surface where adversaries need only reach the highest-value control path or the weakest subsidiary boundary.

The group’s most exposure-heavy assets are offshore gas and energy-trading systems, SCADA and remote monitoring gateways, remote access for field operations, identity and privileged access management, and the integration layer around the German acquisition. For a holding company with essential and important entities in multiple jurisdictions, the main strategic issue is not whether an attacker can reach one system; it is whether Nordstrøm can detect, contain, and govern cross-entity compromise before it converts into regulatory, financial, or operational harm.

## Subsidiary sub-profiles

### Norway (offshore gas)
| Threat actor | Asset exposure | Technique relevance | Severity | Regulatory dimension |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| State-aligned intrusion actor; ransomware operator with operational focus | Offshore gas production systems, remote terminal units, process control gateways, operator workstations, production telemetry | APT-like actor targeting SCADA interfaces and process data links: T0883 (Execute Code via Remote Services) or T0806 (Remote Services) against remote operator gateways; T0872 (Manipulate I/O Image) or T0834 (Man in the Middle) against process data flows. Relevant because Norway’s offshore gas estate depends on remote access and telemetry integrity; a forged or manipulated control signal can alter production visibility and operational trust without immediately triggering obvious IT alarms. | Critical | Norway’s energy sector is treated as essential under NIS2; disruption or compromise of offshore production can trigger significant sectoral and regulator attention, with obligations around continuity and incident reporting.
| Insider or compromised contractor with remote access | Engineering credentials, vendor VPN access, remote maintenance paths | T1078 (Valid Accounts) using abused contractor or vendor credentials; T1021 (Remote Services) to reach maintenance jump hosts. Relevant because offshore assets often rely on partner-support access and short-lived operational accounts, which are difficult to monitor at scale. | High | Cross-border support dependencies increase governance and audit complexity, particularly when operational access is shared across contractors and vendors. |

### Sweden (hydro and wind)
| Threat actor | Asset exposure | Technique relevance | Severity | Regulatory dimension |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cybercriminal group focused on extortion and disruption | Hydro generation telemetry, operational support systems, commercial balancing systems, utility-owned enterprise IT | T1486 (Data Encrypted for Impact) or T1657 (Financial Theft) against utility control and billing layers; T1048 (Exfiltration Over Alternative Protocol) to move data from operational support to external infrastructure. Relevant because Swedish hydro and wind operators often combine industrial telemetry with commercial operational data, giving attackers both downtime leverage and financial extortion opportunities. | High | Swedish NIS2 implementation increases the importance of continuity and governance, especially where generation output and balancing data are tied to national system reliability.
| Industrial espionage actor | Engineering drawings, turbine performance data, grid balancing analytics | T1039 (Data from Network Shared Drive) or T1114 (Email Collection) against design and engineering collaboration tools; T1560 (Archive Collected Data) to exfiltrate operational blueprints and turbine performance information. Relevant because renewable generation assets and their performance data are commercially sensitive and may support strategic competitors or state-backed interests. | Medium-High | Regulatory interest is material but typically framed around operational resilience and sector confidentiality rather than direct critical-infrastructure shutdown. |

### Denmark (offshore wind and trading)

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

### Netherlands (gas and hydrogen infrastructure)
| Threat actor | Asset exposure | Technique relevance | Severity | Regulatory dimension |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nation-state or infrastructure-focused actor | Gas transmission and storage control systems, pipeline telemetry, hydrogen process operations, utility network links | T0856 (Setpoint Modification) or T0810 (Denial of Service) against pipeline control logic; T0836 (Network Connection Enumeration) to map process networks; T0840 (Network Sniffing) to collect industrial protocol data. ### Germany (integration)
| Threat actor | Asset exposure | Technique relevance | Severity | Regulatory dimension |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Initial-access broker or opportunistic ransomware actor | Recently acquired solar portfolio, integration servers, identity federation, shared SaaS services, backup and monitoring tools | T1190 (Exploit Public-Facing Application) or T1566 (Phishing) leading to T1078 (Valid Accounts) on newly integrated SaaS and admin portals; T1556 (Modify Authentication Process) against federated identity if integration is immature. Relevant because the German subsidiary is mid-integration, with incomplete trust boundaries and a moving control baseline; attackers will likely target the seams between legacy and acquired systems rather than the fully mature central estate. | Critical | Germany’s NIS2 implementation adds high urgency because the acquired utility is likely to be in an essential or important sector context while its integration state is still changing; the combined effect is a governance and assurance challenge.
| Third-party or supplier-initiated compromise | Vendor portal access, managed service connections, remote monitoring tools, asset inventories | T1195 (Supply Chain Compromise) or T1199 (Trusted Relationship) to leverage a partner connection into the newly integrated environment. Relevant because a recent acquisition often creates a rush of new integrations, temporary trust relationships, and unmanaged credentials, which adversaries can exploit before the control model stabilises. | High | The regulatory dimension is elevated because the acquisition is still in progress and the firm may be inheriting control or evidence gaps that no single jurisdictional rule fully resolves.
| Insider threat connected to transitional integration work | IAM rules, shared directory structures, migration projects, privileged admin groups | T1078 (Valid Accounts) and T1098 (Account Manipulation) against local admin or service accounts created during integration. Relevant because integration projects frequently create over-privileged accounts, orphaned service credentials, and temporary administrative rights that persist beyond the migration window. | High | This is a major governance issue because integration-phase privilege drift can undermine post-acquisition assurance and increase the chance of delayed detection or misattributed incidents. |
