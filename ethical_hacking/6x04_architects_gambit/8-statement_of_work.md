# Statement of Work, Vanguard Security x Nordstrøm Power Group

## Parties
This Statement of Work (the “SOW”) is entered into by and between Vanguard Security (“Vanguard”) and Nordstrøm Power Group, acting through its authorised representatives, together with the relevant operating subsidiaries participating in the engagement (collectively, the “Client”).

The engagement concerns a multi-subsidiary strategic cyber security review, phased to support the Client’s board-level planning and risk prioritisation across Norway, Sweden, Denmark, the Netherlands and Germany.

Vanguard will provide consulting services in connection with a phased security assessment, architecture review, risk prioritisation, and executive decision-support programme. The Client will provide timely access to personnel, system information, documentation, and approvals required to complete the agreed scope.

## Scope
This SOW covers a phased engagement designed to provide:
- a strategic baseline and threat profile for the holding and its subsidiaries;
- a board-usable view of the group’s cyber risk concentration and dependency exposure;
- a risk-prioritised technical and governance review of the agreed digital and OT-adjacent surfaces;
- a phased recommendation package supporting the Client’s three-year strategic plan and internal decision-making framework;
- a practical roadmap and action plan for the highest-priority risk areas, including the German integration seam.

The services are intentionally phased to create value before broad technical testing. The work will focus on the agreed test perimeter, material control seams, identity and access architecture, third-party dependencies, and the high-risk assets most relevant to board decisions and NIS2-aligned governance.

## Exclusions and assumptions
### Exclusions
- Full physical or industrial process testing outside the approved perimeter.
- Destructive exploitation, live operational disruption testing, or high-risk OT actions beyond the agreed limits.
- Full forensic review of every legacy deployment or historical incident without explicit additional authorisation.
- Provision of legal, regulatory, or compliance representation before a local regulator or in a formal enforcement context.
- Development, procurement, deployment, or warranty of remediation controls.
- Broad social engineering or prolonged adversary emulation beyond the agreed scope.
- Continuous monitoring deployment, managed SOC build-out, or internal organisational redesign.

### Assumptions
- The Client will provide timely access to relevant personnel and governing stakeholders.
- The Client will confirm the relevant in-scope systems, entities, and contact points before each phase begins.
- Local subsidiary teams will provide internal coordination and project sponsorship for review access.
- No material change in business structure, asset ownership, or control regime will be introduced during the engagement without a formal scope re-baselining.
- The Client will not require Vanguard to assume operational responsibility for live production systems or critical infrastructure during testing.
- The German subsidiary integration will remain a distinct risk domain until evidence supports a stable control baseline.

## Deliverables
Vanguard will provide the following deliverables under this SOW:
- Strategic risk and threat profile for the group and each subsidiary
- Clarification questions pack to support the Client’s pre-decision scoping
- Scope architecture and phased engagement design
- Methodology adaptation document for PTES and complementary frameworks
- Resource plan and engagement staffing model
- Engagement risk register
- Final board-ready strategic cyber risk assessment and recommendations package

The exact format, depth, and review cycle for each deliverable will be agreed at the start of each phase and recorded in the relevant phase brief or work order.

## Milestones and timeline
The engagement will proceed in four phases:

1. Phase 1 — Strategic baseline and risk framing
   - Duration: approximately 3 weeks
   - Deliverables: initial baseline, threat model, risk framing, scope architecture refinement

2. Phase 2 — Perimeter validation and evidence collection
   - Duration: approximately 5 weeks
   - Deliverables: validated attack-surface map, asset and dependency review, selected technical validation, initial risk concentration analysis

3. Phase 3 — Targeted deep dive on material subsidiaries and critical domains
   - Duration: approximately 6 weeks
   - Deliverables: subsidiary-specific assessments, critical finding narratives, escalation-ready evidence, prioritised remediation discussion

4. Phase 4 — Roadmap, governance and executive package
   - Duration: approximately 3 weeks
   - Deliverables: final strategic recommendations, governance model, board package, remediation roadmap

The overall engagement timeline is expected to be approximately 17 weeks from the start of Phase 1, subject to dependency sequencing, local access approvals, and the Client’s internal review cycles.

## Team
The team composition for this engagement is as follows:
- 1 Engagement Lead / Principal Consultant
- 1 Senior Security Architect / Methodology Lead
- 1 Senior Offensive Security Engineer
- 1 OT Security Specialist
- 1 Identity and Access / Cloud Security Consultant
- 1 NIS2 / Regulatory Consultant
- 1 German-language Integration Analyst
- 1 Scandinavian Interface Advisor
- 1 Junior Security Consultant
- 1 Analyst / Project Coordinator

The team is structured to meet cross-subsidiary, multi-jurisdictional, OT-aware, and board-facing requirements. Vanguard will ensure that each resource allocated to the engagement has the relevant skill set and experience for the assigned phase and task.

## Pricing
The fees for the engagement are structured as a phased fixed-fee model, with a defined scope for each phase and a fixed schedule of deliverables. This pricing model is appropriate because:
- the engagement is explicitly phased and value-based;
- the Client requires strategic decision support before broad technical execution;
- the work benefits from early scoping discipline and a controlled per-phase budget;
- the commercial model reduces ambiguity and supports board-level governance over a fixed period.

The total fee for the engagement is EUR 800,000, subject to the phase structure set out above and the formal change management process in this SOW.

### Phase pricing
- Phase 1: EUR 140,000
- Phase 2: EUR 240,000
- Phase 3: EUR 280,000
- Phase 4: EUR 140,000

Any additional work outside the agreed scope will require a written change request under Section Change Management.

## Payment terms
- 20% upfront upon signature of this SOW
- 30% on approval of Phase 1 completion and acceptance of the Phase 1 deliverables
- 25% on approval of Phase 2 completion and acceptance of the Phase 2 deliverables
- 15% on approval of Phase 3 completion and acceptance of the Phase 3 deliverables
- 10% on approval of Phase 4 completion and acceptance of the final strategic package

Invoices are due within 30 days of receipt. Late payments shall accrue interest at 1.5% per month or the maximum lawful rate, whichever is lower.

## Change management
Any changes to the agreed scope, schedule, resource model, or deliverables must be approved in writing by both parties. A change may arise from: a request for additional subsidiary review beyond the agreed perimeter; a new discovery that materially expands the technical surface; a regulatory or legal constraint requiring a different operating model; or unplanned delays caused by the Client’s internal process or prioritisation.

Upon identification of a material change, Vanguard will provide a written impact assessment covering:
- the reason for the change;
- the new or expanded scope;
- the estimated time and resource impact;
- the revised fee and payment schedule;
- the effect on milestone timing.

No change is effective until formally approved by an authorised representative of both parties.

## Confidentiality and IP
Vanguard and the Client will protect each other’s confidential information in accordance with the confidentiality obligations set out in the master services agreement or, if no such agreement exists, in a mutually signed confidentiality undertaking prepared before commencement of the engagement.

The Client will retain ownership of its data, systems, and business information. Vanguard will retain ownership of its tools, methodologies, templates, general knowledge, and know-how, except where the parties expressly agree that specific deliverables are to be transferred to the Client.

The deliverables developed specifically for the Client under this engagement will be assigned to the Client on final payment, subject to any written carve-out for Vanguard’s proprietary methods and templates used in the creation of those deliverables.

## Liability and limitation
Vanguard’s liability arising out of this SOW shall be limited to the fees actually paid under the relevant phase or, if an alternative contractual cap is agreed as part of a master agreement, the lower of that cap or the fees paid for the work giving rise to the claim.

Vanguard shall not be liable for indirect, consequential, incidental, punitive, or special damages, including loss of business, reputational harm, or loss of production, unless caused by gross negligence, fraud, or wilful misconduct.

The Client acknowledges that cyber security consultancy is inherently based on assessments, evidence review, and professional judgement and cannot guarantee zero risk or absolute security. Vanguard’s responsibility is to provide a reasonable professional standard of service consistent with the agreed scope and applicable professional practice.

## Termination
Either party may terminate this SOW for material breach if the other party fails to cure such breach within 30 days after notice. The Client may also terminate for convenience on 30 days’ written notice, subject to payment for work performed and non-cancelable commitments made in good faith.

Upon termination, Vanguard will cease work promptly and will provide the Client with all materials and work product created up to the termination date, subject to payment of all outstanding fees and any non-cancelable commitments.

## Governing law and dispute resolution
This SOW shall be governed by and construed in accordance with the laws of the Netherlands, without prejudice to the application of mandatory local laws of the relevant jurisdiction in which the services are performed or the relevant entity is established where those laws provide stronger protection to a local entity or local operations.

This multi-jurisdictional governance clause is essential in a group engagement spanning Norway, Sweden, Denmark, the Netherlands and Germany. The Client’s subsidiaries operate across multiple legal systems and the services may be performed in a way that affects entities governed by different national implementations of NIS2, corporate law, data protection obligations and local contracting practice. The parties therefore agree that:
- the contract shall be interpreted under the laws of the Netherlands as the governing law for the group-level commercial framework;
- any local contracting or operational issues affecting a specific subsidiary shall be governed by the relevant local law only to the extent required by mandatory local rules or the specific local agreement governing that subsidiary;
- the parties will seek good-faith resolution of disputes through executive negotiation within 15 business days of written notice;
- if unresolved, the dispute shall be submitted to binding arbitration under the ICC International Court of Arbitration rules, seated in Amsterdam, with a single arbitrator experienced in technology, cyber security, or infrastructure services;
- the parties agree that the arbitrator may consider the practical and cross-border nature of the services and the need to preserve continuity of the Client’s operations and governance obligations.

This clause is intended to provide a stable commercial framework for a cross-border engagement while preserving the ability to reason with local legal realities where they are mandatory. The parties acknowledge that the exchange of sensitive operational and regulatory information across jurisdictions requires a dispute framework that is predictable, efficient, and sufficiently neutral to the group’s multinational structure.
