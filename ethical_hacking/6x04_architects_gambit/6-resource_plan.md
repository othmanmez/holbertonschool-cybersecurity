# Resource Plan, Nordstrøm Power Group

## Team composition (count, seniority, skills)

### Core team
1. Engagement lead / principal consultant — 1
   - Senior-level architecture and client-facing leadership
   - Experience in board-level risk communication, multi-entity security strategy, and complex client management
   - Oversees the group strategy, scope validation, commercial governance, and final report framing

2. Senior security architect / methodology lead — 1
   - Strong experience in PTES adaptation, multi-jurisdictional cyber risk assessment, and control governance
   - Leads design integrity, risk-first sequencing, and technical-to-business translation

3. Senior offensive security engineer — 1
   - IT and application testing capability, with a solid understanding of identity, remote access, web, and API validation
   - Leads evidence collection and testing on the in-scope digital surfaces

4. OT security specialist — 1
   - OT-aware, ATT&CK for ICS-oriented practitioner
   - Experience with industrial control environments, remote operations, process support dependencies, and operational continuity concerns
   - Essential for Norway, Netherlands, Denmark, and the OT-adjacent elements of Sweden

5. Identity and access / cloud security consultant — 1
   - Experience with IAM, privileged access, federation, SaaS integration, and trust-boundary analysis
   - Critical for the German acquisition and the group’s cross-subsidiary access design

6. Regulatory and NIS2 consultant — 1
   - Multi-country regulatory knowledge, with NIS2 familiarity and sectoral exposure
   - Supports the group-level treatment of essential and important entity classifications and jurisdictional implications

7. German-language specialist / integration analyst — 1
   - Fluent German, ideally with experience in M&A integration security or operational technology transitions
   - Particularly important for the German subsidiary and for communication with local stakeholders and prior-audit artifacts

8. Scandinavian interface advisor — 1
   - Preferred Scandinavian language capability (Swedish, Norwegian, Danish) for the holding interface and stakeholder communication
   - Useful for working with local executive and operational teams without losing nuance in translation

### Support capacity
9. Junior security consultant — 1
   - Assists with evidence collection, documentation, technical synthesis, and action-log maintenance
   - Supports the reporting pipeline and helps keep the project within the board deadline

10. Analyst / project coordinator — 1
   - Maintains action logs, schedule tracking, evidence collation, and internal governance controls
   - Helps keep the engagement disciplined and commercially defensible

### Total core team
- 10 consultants total
- Seniority mix: 6 senior / experienced specialists, 2 mid-level specialists, 2 support roles
- Required skills summary:
  - OT-aware security assessment
  - NIS2 and cross-border regulatory awareness
  - German-language support for the German subsidiary
  - Scandinavian-language preference for the holding interface
  - identity and trust-boundary review
  - board-level reporting and strategic communication

## Weekly allocation and total consultant-days

### Allocation model
- Phase 1 (weeks 1–3): 6–7 consultants active, average 50–60% utilisation per person, focused on scoping, strategic review, and risk framing
- Phase 2 (weeks 4–8): 8–10 consultants active, with heavier use of technical and OT specialists; average 70–80% utilisation
- Phase 3 (weeks 9–14): 8 consultants active, weighted toward OT, IAM, and regulatory specialists; average 75–85% utilisation
- Phase 4 (weeks 15–17): 5–6 consultants active, with leadership, governance, and reporting concentration; average 60–70% utilisation

### Indicative total consultant-days
Estimated total: 560–650 consultant-days

Approximate allocation by role:
- Engagement lead: 25–30 days
- Senior security architect: 35–45 days
- Senior offensive security engineer: 60–75 days
- OT security specialist: 60–75 days
- Identity and access specialist: 45–55 days
- Regulatory and NIS2 consultant: 40–50 days
- German-language integration analyst: 30–40 days
- Scandinavian interface advisor: 20–30 days

This is a realistic commercial staffing level for a group-level energy risk review with five subsidiaries, a 90-day board window, and a recent acquisition in Germany. It is broad enough to include the required disciplines without overcommitting to a large, multi-month field team that would be disproportionate to the retainer signal.

## External specialists (in / out, with rationale)

### In: Legal NIS2 advisor (specialist support)
This is recommended as a short-term external specialist engagement, not as a core team member. Nordstrøm operates across multiple jurisdictions and different essential/important classifications, and the client’s board will want a defensible view of what the legal and regulatory exposure means in practice. A specialist legal advisor is useful to calibrate the NIS2 mapping, document the risk interpretation, and ensure the governance recommendations are aligned with national implementation realities. In this client, the legal/regulatory lens is materially different from technical security advice and should not be approximated by the core team.

### In: OT engineer subcontractor (specialist support)
A subcontracted OT engineer is justified because the Norwegian offshore gas, Dutch infrastructure, and Danish offshore wind contexts are not generic IT environments. The OT specialist on the core team can lead the assessment, but an external OT engineering partner is valuable for deep dependency and process-control understanding, especially where the engineering and operations environment is highly specialised. This is particularly relevant for remote maintenance interfaces, telemetry integrity, and process-support points that require engineering knowledge beyond that of a general pentester or security architect.

### Out: Broad-spectrum external M&A due-diligence firm for the German acquisition
This should generally be excluded from the core scope unless the client specifically asks for a post-acquisition diligence overlay. The VANGUARD requirement is to assess the security posture of the German integration and its control seams, not to take over the legal or financial diligence function of the transaction. A separate diligence firm would overlap with the work and create conflict and duplication without materially improving the specific cyber engagement value.

### Out: Full-time public relations or crisis communications advisor
This is outside the engagement’s technical architecture unless the client specifically wants risk-communication support as part of a broader board advisory package. The board pressure is real, but the value here is in the technical and governance framing, not in a full crisis communications operation. The engagement should not widen into a PR function unless that is explicitly approved as a separate scope line.
