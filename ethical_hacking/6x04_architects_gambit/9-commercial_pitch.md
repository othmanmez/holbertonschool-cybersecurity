# Commercial Pitch, Nordstrøm Power Group

## Commercial value
This is an attractive engagement because it is a high-value, high-trust account with clear strategic upside and a realistic path to repeat business. The proposed work is a fixed-fee engagement of 800,000 EUR across a disciplined four-phase model, with a strong commercial logic: the first phase quickly establishes risk framing and board-level relevance, while the later phases deepen only where the evidence justifies it. That creates a credible service offering without opening the door to uncontrolled scope expansion.

The value is not only the initial retainer. Nordstrøm is a Scandinavian energy holding with operations across Norway, Sweden, Denmark, the Netherlands and Germany, which makes it a strategically relevant account for Vanguard in the European energy sector. If we perform well, this is precisely the kind of client that can lead to broader strategic advisory work, sector-specific security reviews across subsidiaries, and a long-term relationship as NIS2 obligations and infrastructure complexity continue to rise. In commercial terms, this is a real account with board pressure, a clear decision window, and enough complexity to establish Vanguard’s credibility in a market where many firms still sell generic “energy security” packages without understanding the operational reality.

## Risk profile and mitigation
The risks to the engagement itself are real, and our design addresses them directly. Scope creep is limited by a phased, evidence-based structure with a hard change-control process and documented exclusions. Conflict-of-interest risk is reduced through a formal review of prior work and third-party dependencies, particularly in the German integration context where an overlapping prior audit could distort the evidence picture. Timing risk is managed by a milestone-based plan with phased sequencing and escalation triggers, so the board deadline is treated as a delivery driver rather than a surprise. OT testing risk is mitigated by strict perimeter control, engineering sign-off, and agreed stop conditions before any live process-adjacent environment is touched. Subsidiary cooperation risk is addressed through group-level sponsorship and local stakeholder alignment, while legal risk across multiple jurisdictions is reduced by a multi-jurisdictional governance model with explicit dispute and evidence-handling principles.

Our design does not ignore risk; it converts the project’s risk profile into a managed execution model. That matters to the committee because it reduces the likelihood that this becomes a client-failure project or a consultancy-failure project.

## Why this design, and confidence in execution
The obvious alternative is a broader and cheaper “do everything” proposal: an all-encompassing assessment promised in a single pass. That is the model that creates scope runaways, weak commercial discipline, and poor delivery outcomes. Our design does the opposite. It is shaped by the architect choices made in the earlier tasks: requalifying the brief through clarification questions, phasing value by risk concentration, and refusing to price generic exhaustion rather than target value. The methodology adapts PTES to the client’s actual context, uses ATT&CK and ATT&CK for ICS where the operational reality demands it, and keeps NIST 800-53 and NIS2 mapping where the board needs governance language rather than technical noise.

This is why we have confidence in execution. The scope is narrowed to what moves the board decision, the staffing is tailored to the risk domains, and the legal-commercial structure is written to protect both the client and the firm. We are not selling a broad field exercise; we are selling a disciplined, defensible engagement that matches the client’s risk surface, its board timeline, and its commercial reality. That is the right proposal to send.
