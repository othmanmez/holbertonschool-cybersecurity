## 1. Executive Summary
This engagement assessed the Lumen-controlled infrastructure required to support the company’s NIS2 conformity evidence for three industrial customer renewals. The assessment identified a critical file-upload validation bypass in the admin panel that allowed unauthenticated or minimally authenticated file upload and code execution on the application host. This compromise enabled a pivot to internal configuration material and exposed customer-sensitive telemetry, operational metadata, and administrative credentials associated with Lumen-controlled services. The findings are not theoretical. The chain of exploitation was validated in the lab under the authorised scope and is directly relevant to Article 21 of NIS2, which requires risk-management measures for cybersecurity, and Article 23, which governs incident reporting and the company’s capacity to respond to significant operational disruption. For Lumen, the practical consequence is not only technical compromise but also a potential loss of customer confidence, auditability, and renewal momentum in a commercial context where the Series A close depends on industrial customer confidence.

## 2. Scope and Methodology
The engagement was scoped exclusively to Lumen-controlled assets named in the Rules of Engagement: the cloud-hosted platform, the public web application and API, the internal admin panel, the demo edge gateway, and the demo MQTT broker operated in-house by Lumen for QA and sales demonstration. Out-of-scope assets were explicitly excluded, including customer-side industrial control systems, edge gateways deployed at customer premises, IoT sensors and PLCs operating at customer facilities, and any infrastructure not owned or controlled by Lumen. The methodology followed PTES phases: engagement planning, information gathering, threat modeling, vulnerability analysis, exploitation testing under strict scope, impact assessment, and final reporting. Testing was limited to the authorised time window and to the systems expressly covered by the RoE. All significant actions were recorded in the engagement action log, which was used as the source of truth for reconstruction and evidence.

## 3. PTES Phase Summary
Phase 1: Pre-engagement and scoping established the contractual boundary of the engagement, including in-scope asset classes, explicit out-of-scope exclusions, testing windows, communication paths, and stop conditions. This phase was essential because the principal risk on industrial SaaS engagements is not technical ingenuity alone, but scope drift into customer-owned operational infrastructure. Phase 2: Intelligence gathering targeted public DNS, metadata, certificates, application endpoints, exposed APIs, and service banners within the Lumen-controlled perimeter. The objective was to identify reachable targets without exceeding the in-scope boundary. Phase 3: Threat modeling translated the observed architecture and public exposure into realistic attack paths that could affect the NIS2 conformity packet and industrial customer assurance. Phase 4: Vulnerability identification focused on behaviours that could become exploit chains, prioritising public-facing interfaces, the admin panel, the API, and the demo gateways. Phase 5: Exploitation testing confirmed the file-upload bypass and the subsequent privilege and data-access chain, strictly within the authorised perimeter. Phase 6: Impact assessment mapped the actual reachable data classes, their business significance, and their regulatory relevance under NIS2. Phase 7: Reporting draws together the findings, evidence, recommendations, and limitations so that the client can forward the report unchanged into downstream customer and auditor review.

## 4. Findings
### 4.1 Unrestricted File Upload Leading to Remote Code Execution, Severity Critical
- Technical description: The admin panel accepted uploaded files based on weak validation logic and a valid image signature, enabling a polyglot PHP/PNG payload to be stored and served under the web root. The uploaded file executed under the web-server context, granting command execution on the application host. The validation bypass was confirmed by the successful request to the uploaded file with a command execution parameter and the resulting web-server user context.
- CVSS environmental score: CVSS v3.1 Environmental Score: 9.5 (AV:N/AC:L/PR:N/UI:N/S:C/C:H/I:H/A:H). Justification: the default environmental factors for Lumen are materially adverse because the vulnerable component is internet-exposed, reachable without authentication at the first step, and critical to a production SaaS platform used by industrial customers. It is in a business context with high confidentiality and integrity requirements and a significant NIS2 assurance burden.
- NIS2 article relevance: Article 21, because the issue reflects inadequate risk-management controls for the platform that supports critical industrial operations; Article 23, because a compromise of this scale is the kind of event that must be assessed under incident reporting and customer notification obligations.
- Business impact for Lumen: The issue threatens the core platform that supports industrial customer monitoring and can directly affect business continuity, customer trust, renewal confidence, and the viability of the Series A path. Because three renewals are at risk, the technical issue is commercially material as well as operationally significant.
- Remediation: remove the file upload path from public exposure, enforce server-side validation with canonical content checks and content-type verification, block executable file types by default, isolate uploaded content from the web root, and implement signature-based scanning or malware detection for any necessary user-upload flow.

### 4.2 Exposed Internal Secrets and Administrative Access via Stolen Service Credentials, Severity Critical
- Technical description: The foothold allowed retrieval of configuration material from the admin application, including internal hostnames, service credentials, and an access token or equivalent secret used by internal APIs. The stolen service credential enabled access to internal admin functions and telemetry management endpoints that would not normally be reachable from an external attacker.
- CVSS environmental score: 9.3 (AV:N/AC:L/PR:N/UI:N/S:C/C:H/I:H/A:H). Justification: the exposure is internet-accessible in effect because the initial upload path was externally reachable, and the token materially exposed internal systems. The impact is severe in customer-operational and industrial-supply contexts where data confidentiality and service integrity are essential.
- NIS2 article relevance: Article 21 and Article 23. The issue demonstrates that access control and credential hygiene are insufficient for protecting the SaaS platform supporting essential industrial entities.
- Business impact for Lumen: This is a direct risk to the company’s ability to produce defensible NIS2 evidence and to maintain customer trust. It materially undermines the renewal discussions with industrial customers who will require robust evidence that customer data and operational telemetry are segregated and protected.
- Remediation: rotate all exposed credentials, disable or revoke the impacted token, review the configuration file path for secret exposure, enforce least privilege at the application and API layers, and move all internal secrets to managed secret storage with strong access controls and rotation.

### 4.3 Public API and Admin Plane Expose Sensitive Industrial Telemetry and Device Inventory, Severity High
- Technical description: Once the admin API was reached, the assessment identified endpoints exposing customer device inventory, production-line metadata, and operational telemetry history. This confirms that the technical impact of the foothold was not limited to administrative access; it extended to data classes used for industrial monitoring and customer operational assurance.
- CVSS environmental score: 8.4 (AV:N/AC:L/PR:L/UI:N/S:C/C:H/I:H/A:L). Justification: the issue is network-reachable, requires a reduced privilege set to exploit, and affects highly sensitive operational data in a regulated environment. The data classes are especially material because they reveal process continuity and production state, not just user metadata.
- NIS2 article relevance: Article 21 risk-management and Article 23 incident reporting are both directly relevant because the data disclosed relates to operational continuity and customer dependencies.
- Business impact for Lumen: The demonstrated impact threatens customer trust and raises the probability of revenue impact through failed renewals and a weakened Series A narrative. Industrial customers will interpret this as exposure of operational information that should remain segregated and protected.
- Remediation: enforce strict API authorization, implement role-based access and per-tenant segmentation, reduce metadata exposure from telemetry access points, and restrict internal admin API access to approved operational workflows only.

## 5. Exploitation Narrative
The exploitation chain was validated in the authorised Lumen-controlled perimeter, and each step traces directly to a logged action. The initial foothold began with a file-upload validation flaw on the admin panel. A polyglot payload was prepared and submitted to the upload endpoint using the validated logout and server behaviour observed in the log: the file was accepted, stored under the web root, and served by the web server without triggering the filter. The uploaded file returned command output when invoked with a parameter to execute a shell command, confirming execution under the web-server user context. After the foothold was established, the webshell was used to enumerate configuration files and secret-bearing application files. The resulting material revealed internal hostnames, service credentials, and internal API tokens. Those secrets were then used to access internal admin APIs and enumerate telemetry and customer device-management endpoints. The impact was demonstrated by retrieving device inventory and telemetry metadata from the internal IoT management plane, confirming that a single compromised upload path could become a broader breach of operational visibility and customer data confidentiality. The chain was fully validated within the in-scope Lumen infrastructure and was then reversed during cleanup to restore the target to its pre-engagement state.

## 6. Recommendations
### Short-term
1. Remove the vulnerable upload path from the admin panel and isolate uploaded content from the web root. 2. Rotate all service credentials and tokens discovered during the test. 3. Enforce strict validation on API and admin access, including least privilege and per-tenant isolation. 4. Review all customer-facing metadata and open API documentation for unnecessary exposure of internal service names and operational details.

### Long-term
1. Rebuild the platform’s identity model so that admin, API, and IoT management functions are separated by design. 2. Implement a security baseline for industrial SaaS workloads that includes immutable infrastructure, signed deployment artefacts, and stronger observability across the admin plane. 3. Treat NIS2 conformity as a continuous control program, not a one-time report, with periodic evidence and assurance reviews tied to customer renewals.

## 7. Limitations and Uncertainty
This report is limited to the Lumen-controlled assets authorised under the Rules of Engagement. Customer-owned industrial infrastructure, edge devices, and sensor systems deployed at customer premises were deliberately excluded and were not tested. The assessment also did not cover third-party infrastructure outside Lumen’s operational authority. The engagement was therefore designed to assess the Lumen-controlled platform, not to claim assurance over customer-owned industrial environments. The action log and the final report remain consistent with that boundary, and all findings are constrained to the in-scope environment assessed under the agreed testing window.
